# Learning

What learning science says about how students learn from course material. Read it when judging whether pages and exercises support learning, or when the teacher asks why a teaching choice works.

## What to check

- **Recommendation stated as a finding**: text (or your own suggestion) that turns a research finding into a fixed rule, such as "three quizzes per chapter". Say what the evidence supports and leave the amount to the teacher. [Carpenter-2022]
- **Misconceptions left standing**: a page that teaches a concept where a common wrong idea exists but never addresses it. Suggest an example or question that brings the wrong idea out. Students' earlier ideas get refined, not simply replaced. [HPL-2018] [Smith-1994]
- **Unnecessary load for beginners**: several new ideas introduced at once, essential steps left implicit, or an explanation away from what it explains (`course-material/references/guidance/course-design.md` **Decorative or distant visuals**). Suggest splitting the page or adding the missing step. Don't remove difficulty that is the point of the task. [Sweller-2019]
- **Novices left to discover complex procedures alone**: a first exercise on a new procedure with no worked example or guidance. Suggest a worked example first, then practice with gradually less support. Asking students to try a problem before instruction can also work when instruction then builds on their attempts; say which design the page seems to follow. [Kirschner-2006] [Sinha-2021] [Atkinson-2000]
- **Support that never fades**: every exercise in a chapter repeats the fully worked pattern. Guidance that helps novices can hinder learners who already know the material; suggest later exercises with less support. [Kalyuga-2007]
- **No retrieval practice**: a chapter where students only read, with nothing asking them to recall or apply what they read. Suggest short questions after key ideas, with feedback. Short factual questions are a legitimate form of this. [Yang-2021] [Adesope-2017] [Dunlosky-2013]
- **Practice massed in one place**: an important idea practised only on the page that teaches it. Suggest revisiting it in later chapters' exercises. The best gap depends on how long students should remember it. [Cepeda-2006] [Carpenter-2022]
- **Interleaving where it doesn't fit**: mixing problem types is strongest for similar categories students confuse; it is unclear for expository text and worse than blocking for learning words. [Brunmair-2019] [Firth-2021] Mixing whole lessons or long activities isn't established. (editorial)
- **Feedback that only says right or wrong**: exercise feedback with no explanation, feedback that hands over the answer before the student has tried, or praise of the person ("you're a natural") instead of information about the task. Suggest feedback that says what was wrong and how to proceed. [Wisniewski-2020] [Hattie-2007] [Shute-2008]
- **Activity without thinking**: "interactive" elements (click to reveal, drag and drop) that don't require students to generate, explain or decide anything. Suggest a version where students produce something. [Chi-2014] [Freeman-2014]
- **No chance to check one's own understanding**: material where students can't tell whether they could actually do the task. Suggest a self-check: predict, attempt without help, compare. Feeling familiar with a text isn't the same as being able to recall or use it. [Bjork-2013]
- **Generic skills named but never practised**: an objective or course description promising argumentation, writing, collaboration or information search, with no exercise where students practise it in the subject and get feedback, or a course that assumes students arrive with it. Suggest one such exercise, with criteria and feedback; reviewed studies link active, collaborative work, integration into subject courses and continuous feedback with these skills, though mostly from students' self-reports. [Tuononen-2022] [HYPE-2026]
- **Learning styles**: text that matches teaching to "visual", "auditory" or "kinesthetic" learners. Report it as unsupported and suggest choosing the format by what the content needs. [Pashler-2008]

## Background

### Four levels behind a teaching recommendation

| Level | Question | Example: retrieval practice |
|---|---|---|
| Finding | What was observed, with whom, compared with what? | Practice tests beat restudying for later retention across many settings. |
| Explanation | Why might it work? How well supported is that? | Several mechanisms proposed; retrieval also improves later study and self-monitoring. |
| Recommendation | What practice is reasonable here, under which assumptions? | Give students chances to recall important ideas, with feedback. |
| Local evaluation | Does it work for these students and outcomes? | Look at delayed performance and where students struggle. |

Table: (editorial)

The research doesn't fix the number, format or stakes of quizzes; those are the teacher's design decisions. [Carpenter-2022] [Yang-2021]

### Effect sizes and their limits

Classroom quizzing raises achievement by about g = 0.5 on average, more with corrective feedback and matching formats. [Yang-2021] Retrieval practice transfers to new questions (overall d ≈ 0.40), most across formats and to application and inference questions. [Pan-2018] Feedback averages d ≈ 0.48 but varies widely; over a third of feedback interventions in an older meta-analysis lowered performance. [Wisniewski-2020] [Kluger-1996] Active learning in undergraduate STEM raised exam scores and cut failure rates across 225 studies, but "active learning" covers many different activities. [Freeman-2014]

Interleaving helps most when categories are similar to each other and examples within a category vary; benefits extend to delayed tests. [Firth-2021] [Brunmair-2019]

### Feedback types, with programming examples

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Correctness | "Test case 3 failed." |
| Diagnostic | "The function changes a list that the caller still uses." |
| Conceptual | "This compares identity, not equality." |
| Strategy | "Try reproducing it with the smallest input that fails." |
| Reflection | "Compare your first guess with the actual cause." |

Examples: (editorial)

Feedback about the task, the process and the student's own monitoring tends to help more than feedback about the person; effects vary with the information feedback carries. [Hattie-2007] [Wisniewski-2020]

### Frameworks are not guarantees

Universal Design for Learning (UDL 3.0) is a design framework for reducing barriers to engagement, representation and expression. Use it to generate suggestions, not as evidence that a given design improves learning. For course text, its language-and-symbols guideline (terms and notation explained where students need them, the terms kept) and its building-knowledge guideline (prior knowledge and transfer) matter most; its engagement guidelines are in `course-material/references/guidance/engagement.md`, perception is mostly WCAG's, and action and expression is exercise design. [CAST-2024] Learning-styles matching lacks support from studies with the design needed to test it. [Pashler-2008]

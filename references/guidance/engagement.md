# Engagement and motivation

How to tell interest from learning, and how material can keep students going without lowering standards. Read it when writing new teaching material, when reviewing motivating content or course pacing, or when the teacher asks how to keep students engaged.

## What to check

- **Enjoyment treated as evidence of learning**: a course or page that cites satisfaction, likes or "students loved it" as proof it teaches well. Satisfaction ratings and learning are weakly related or unrelated, and students in more demanding active classes can learn more while feeling they learn less. Suggest measuring learning separately. [Uttl-2017] [Deslauriers-2019]
- **Engagement measured by clicks**: views, clicks or time on page reported as engagement. Behavioural, emotional and cognitive engagement are different things; suggest a measure of what students actually did or learned. [Fredricks-2004]
- **No reason to care**: a chapter that opens with definitions and never says what problem the idea solves. Suggest a genuine question or application up front. Interest can be triggered by the situation and grow into lasting interest with support. [Hidi-2006]
- **Settled question presented as open**, or a finding overstated, to create interest. Suggest a real puzzle instead, e.g. "why can two correct algorithms differ greatly in measured speed?". (editorial)
- **Choice, competence, belonging**: material with no meaningful choices, no visible progress, or a tone that makes errors embarrassing. Suggest one concrete change (an optional path, a progress cue, a normalising sentence about common mistakes). Support for autonomy, competence and relatedness sustains motivation. [Ryan-2000] [CAST-2024]
- **Self-paced course with no structure**: no suggested pace, no interim tasks, or all graded work at the end. In one survey of emergency online teaching (one university, 2020), about 15% reported trouble with time management or procrastination, most in a less organised profile. [Haarala-Muhonen-2025] Suggest a weekly pace and small interim tasks with feedback, and deadlines where the course uses them. (editorial)
- **Your suggestions**: where students lack support, suggest scaffolding, hints or worked examples, not an easier task. (editorial)

## Background

### Terms that are related but not interchangeable

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Enjoyment | Positive feelings during the activity |
| Interest | Attention and attraction to the subject; can be situational or lasting |
| Behavioural engagement | Observable participation and effort |
| Cognitive engagement | Mental effort and strategy use |
| Motivation | What starts and sustains goal-directed effort |
| Learning | Change in knowledge or capability |
| Retention | Learning that lasts over time |

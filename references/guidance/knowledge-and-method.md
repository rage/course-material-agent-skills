# Knowledge and method

What kind of claim the material makes and how that kind of claim is justified. Read it when checking claims, explanations of how a field knows things, or text about science, models or method.

## What to check

- **Wrong kind of justification**: a theorem "confirmed by many examples", an empirical claim "proved" by argument alone, a historical claim with no sources. Ask whether the text should name the justification the field uses (proof, evidence, sources, argument). [SEP-Method] [SEP-Math] [SEP-History] An ethical conclusion drawn from facts alone is the same kind of gap. (editorial)
- **"The scientific method" as one fixed procedure**: hypothesis–experiment–conclusion presented as how all science works. Suggest wording that allows observation, modelling, simulation, comparison and synthesis. [SEP-Method]
- **Non-experimental fields called unscientific**, or experiments and numbers treated as proof of reliability. Suggest naming the field's own methods. [SEP-Method]
- **Theory as "just a guess"**, or a theory that "becomes a law" with enough evidence. Laws describe regularities; theories explain them; neither grows into the other. [NAS-2008] [Lederman-2002]
- **Falsifiability as the sole test** of whether something is science. It is one influential criterion with known limits. [SEP-Pseudo] [SEP-Popper]
- **A mistake or unusual hypothesis called pseudoscience**: suggest naming the specific unsupported claims instead of the label. [SEP-Pseudo]
- **Models presented as reality**: a model's assumptions, idealisations or range of use left unstated where a student would apply it outside that range. Suggest one sentence on what the model leaves out. [SEP-Models]
- **Accurate prediction offered as proof of the mechanism**. A model can predict well and still explain wrongly. Suggest separating "predicts" from "explains". [Shmueli-2010]
- **Mathematical consistency offered as proof that a model fits the world**. [SEP-Models]
- **Inference type mislabelled**: an inductive generalisation called deduction, or a best explanation (abduction) called proven. Suggest the accurate word. [SEP-Abduction] [SEP-Induction]
- **Science said to settle value questions**, or to be the only source of knowledge. Suggest separating the empirical part from the value judgement. [Haack-2012]
- **The reverse**: settling an empirical question by intuition, logic or personal experience where evidence exists. Suggest citing the evidence. [SEP-Method]
- **Origin used as justification**: an idea dismissed or accepted because of where it came from (a dream, an analogy, an outsider) rather than how it was tested. [SEP-Discovery]

How much of this a course should teach depends on its goals: a beginner course needs correct wording, not a lesson in philosophy of science. (editorial)

## Background

Fields justify claims differently, and one investigation often mixes methods. [SEP-Method] Computer science and software engineering: `course-material/references/guidance/computing.md`.

| Field | Main ways of justifying claims |
|---|---|
| Mathematics | Definitions, axioms, deductive proof [SEP-Math] |
| Natural sciences | Observation, experiment, modelling, statistical inference [SEP-Method] |
| Historical sciences and history | Source criticism, reconstruction, comparative evidence [SEP-History] |
| Philosophy | Logical argument, conceptual analysis (editorial) |
| Ethics | Normative argument informed by empirical facts (editorial) |

Forms of inference a course may rely on:

| Inference | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Deduction | Conclusions that follow necessarily from premises [SEP-Consequence] |
| Induction | Generalisation beyond observed cases, never certain [SEP-Induction] |
| Abduction | The best available explanation, revisable [SEP-Abduction] |
| Statistical inference | Conclusions from data under model assumptions, with stated uncertainty [SEP-Statistics] |
| Causal inference | Whether changing one factor changes another; needs more than association [Hernan-WhatIf] |
| Decision under uncertainty | A choice weighing outcomes and their probabilities [SEP-Decision] |

Few philosophers still defend a single unified scientific method; how much methods differ between fields is debated. [SEP-Method] Astronomy and geology work largely without manipulating their objects and are still science. (editorial)

A hypothesis is a testable proposal; a theory is a well-supported explanatory framework; a law states a regularity, often mathematically. Students commonly hold the "theories become laws" misconception. [NAS-2008] [Lederman-2002]

Popper's falsifiability criterion has been criticised both for excluding legitimate science and for admitting some pseudoscience. A mistake or an unconventional hypothesis isn't pseudoscience; the warning sign is a pattern: claims presented as science that lack its reliability, resist correction, or can be read as confirmed by any outcome. [SEP-Pseudo]

Models are selective representations built on assumptions, idealisations and approximations; their usefulness depends on the purpose and the range they were built for. [SEP-Models]

Where an idea comes from (context of discovery) is separate from whether it is justified (context of justification), though philosophers debate how sharp that line is. [SEP-Discovery]

# Honesty about uncertainty and limits

Whether the material states its confidence, scope, limits and counterevidence fairly, and how it handles corrections. Read it when checking claims, summaries of research, "always/never" statements, or a course's way of reporting and fixing errors.

## What to check

- **Confidence that doesn't match the evidence**: "proven", "always", "never", "the best" on claims that are preliminary, contested or context-dependent; or hedging on well-established knowledge. Suggest wording that matches the evidence. [vanderBles-2019]
- **Established and tentative mixed together**: an early finding stated in the same voice as textbook knowledge. Suggest marking which is which. [NASEM-2019]
- **Counterevidence left out**: one side of a live research question presented as settled, or well-known contrary results not mentioned. Suggest a sentence on the other results. [Cook-2018]
- **Unstated scope**: a result stated without the population, setting or conditions it was found in, where students might apply it elsewhere. Suggest stating where it was found. [Henrich-2010] [Pound-2004]
- **Stronger than the source**: the text's claim goes beyond what the cited source says (causal from correlational, general from specific, certain from tentative), or a citation chain amplifies a claim no source supports. Suggest the source's own wording; the `course-material-claim-check` skill checks each source against its sentence. [Sumner-2014] [Greenberg-2009]
- **Undisclosed ties**: a product or vendor promoted without saying how the course relates to it. Suggest a disclosure line. [ICMJE]
- **Theory vs practice**: something possible in principle presented as practical, or an ideal result presented as what students will get. Suggest stating what students can expect in practice. (editorial)
- **Undated claims, silent fixes**: time-sensitive claims with no date. Where errors were fixed, the change is visible to students who already read the page if it matters to them. [ICMJE] [NASEM-2019]

Uncertainty can be stated briefly: a range or a date is often enough; prefer a number over a verbal hedge where one exists. In experiments, numeric uncertainty lowered readers' trust only slightly; verbal hedges lowered it more. [vanderBles-2020]

## Background

### Kinds of being wrong

| Case | What it is | How to raise it |
|---|---|---|
| Honest mistake | An error, open to correction | Report it with the source (editorial) |
| Legitimate disagreement | Experts differ on a question the evidence doesn't settle | Suggest presenting both positions with their support [Miller-2013] |
| Outdated content | Was right, has since changed | Give the current state and its date [NASEM-2019] |

### Uncertainty

Uncertainty can be about the fact or number itself (stated as a range or a verbal qualifier) or about the quality of the evidence behind it; material may need to say either. [vanderBles-2019]

Scientific confidence comes in degrees: some conclusions rest on many independent lines of evidence, others on one study. Material that treats all claims as equally uncertain misleads as much as material that treats all as certain. [NASEM-2019]

### Generalisation

Results hold under the conditions they were found in. Moving them to a different population, setting, scale or time needs its own justification. Common gaps: lab to real world, animals to people, one country's students to all students, one company's practice to all organisations, one course's result to all courses. [Henrich-2010] [Pound-2004]

### Interests

Industry-sponsored drug and device studies more often report favourable results and conclusions than independent ones, a bias that standard risk-of-bias assessments don't explain. [Lundh-2017]

Journals require authors to disclose financial and other interests that could influence their work, and to correct or retract published errors visibly. [ICMJE]
